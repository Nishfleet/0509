## The bill at 10, 100 and 1,000 brands

Daily cadence, 30-day month, 15 browser-seconds per brand-day:

| Brands | Browser-hours / mo | Over the included 10 h | **Browser Rendering cost** |
| ------ | ------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| 10     | 1.2                | none                   | **$0.00**                  |
| 100    | 12.5               | 2.5 h                  | **$0.22**                  |
| 1,000  | 125.0              | 115 h                  | **$10.35**                 |

D1 writes under the shipped design — one `snapshot` row per watch per tick, ten sources per brand, each snapshot paired in one `batch()` with a `source` latest-facts `UPDATE`:

| Brands | `snapshot` rows / mo | `source` latest-facts rows / mo | D1 rows / mo | Against the 50M included |
| ------ | -------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------ |
| 10     | 3,000                | 3,000                           | 6,000        | 0.012%                   |
| 100    | 30,000               | 30,000                          | 60,000       | 0.12%                    |
| 1,000  | 300,000              | 300,000                         | 600,000      | 1.2%                     |

The paired update is one write attempt per check, and a check whose snapshot is not the source's newest writes nothing. On a source whose watches are all polled in one run (`workers/workflows/mentions.ts` hands every target the same `now`), the watches tie on `fetched_at` and only the first-committed update moves the row, so the shipped figure sits at or below the upper bound above.

**At every scale we plan for, the Cloudflare bill is dominated by Browser Rendering duration, and it is small.** R2 holds the snapshot bodies and has no egress fee. KV holds counters and cursors, comfortably inside its included tier.

That dominance claim is still an estimate. The measured week below can price worker `0509` CPU, and it cannot attribute browser hours to that worker.

## The three design choices that decide the number

1. **Only two legs use a browser.** Mentions and hiring are RSS and JSON APIs at sub-second cost. If a future source reaches for Browser Rendering because it is convenient, it costs roughly 500× the cheap path per call. The keep-list finding stands — arbitrary brand homepages genuinely need the browser — but that is a reason to use it _there_, not everywhere.
2. **The hash gate comes before the screenshot.** An unchanged page costs a fetch and a hash comparison, not a browser session. Skipping this is the difference between 15 browser-seconds per brand-day and 15 per brand-page-day.
3. **Concurrency, not duration, is the cliff.** Duration is $0.09/hour and we use very little of it. Concurrency is **$2.00 per additional concurrent browser, averaged over the month** — so ten extra concurrent browsers left running is $20/mo, roughly a hundred times the duration cost at 100 brands. At 15 s per brand and 10 concurrent browsers, 1,000 brands sweep in about 25 minutes of wall time, entirely inside the included concurrency. **Cap the sweep at 10 concurrent browsers and the browser bill stays near zero.**

## An honest correction to my own schema argument

In `docs/REBUILD-SCHEMA.md` I chose the snapshot-plus-curated design over a row-per-observed-item design partly on cost, citing ~300k D1 writes a month at 100 brands. That number is right, but **D1 includes 50 million writes a month**, so at 100 brands _both_ designs cost $0 in D1. The cost argument as I framed it does not bite at our scale.

The design choice still stands, for reasons that survive the correction: it bounds write growth rather than letting it scale with everything observed, it keeps blobs out of D1 rows entirely, and it is the pattern that does not produce the failure below. But I overstated the near-term saving and the record should say so.

## The $105 anti-pattern, priced

On 2026-09-17 this account's D1 rows-written reached roughly **$105**. At $1.00 per million rows beyond the included 50 million, that is about **155 million rows written in a month** — some 5 million a day.

No design in this document approaches that: the largest here is 600,000 D1 rows a month at 1,000 brands. Reaching 155M means writing per _observed element_ rather than per item — a row per ad impression, per page element, per poll result — which is the pattern the batching rule exists to forbid. The lesson priced: **the gap between "a row per item" and "a row per element" is the gap between $0 and $105.**

## Guardrails, as numbers

- Browser Rendering: the app calls only Quick Actions (`env.BROWSER.quickAction`), which [bill browser hours only](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/pricing/); the concurrent-browser charge applies to Browser Sessions (Puppeteer, Playwright, CDP), which no live code uses. If a Browser Session is ever added, **≤ 10 concurrent browsers**, a config value. Raising it costs $2 per extra concurrent browser per month and needs Nish's deliberate yes, recorded in the PR with the cost. No agent leaves it hanging while customers wait: if a sweep cannot finish in its window at the cap, escalate to Nish the same day with the measured number and a proposed cap, and meanwhile prioritise ON brands' home and pricing pages over long-tail pages. Never drop brands silently. Duration budget ≤ 20 browser-seconds per brand per day.
- D1: no write per observed element. Snapshots are one row per watch per tick, paired in the same `batch()` with the `source` row's latest-facts update; signals are one row per item that survived judgment.
- Blobs — screenshots, raw payloads, HTML — in **R2**, never base64 into a D1 row.
- Hot counters (poll cursors, budgets, tallies) in **KV or a Durable Object**, never a D1 write per increment.
- Budget alerts set at **$10 and $20 of total monthly spend**, so the first surprise arrives as an email rather than as an invoice.

## What the nightly cost guard measures, and what it refuses to

The guard (`app/lib/observability/cost-guard.ts`, called by `runCostGuard`) has two kinds of line. The per-brand lines (`r2_class_a_ops`, `browser_ms_0509`, `ai_calls_0509`) divide the day's usage by the count of ON **competitor** brands — `role = 'competitor' AND state = 'on'`. The workspace's own `self` entity row is not a brand in the divisor: the schema CHECK (`role = 'competitor' OR state = 'on'`) forces every `self` row to `on`, so counting `state = 'on'` alone made the divisor `competitors_on + workspaces_self` and diluted both `measured_per_brand` and the alert's `on_brands` (0509#6086).

The two account-wide lines are absolute and are never divided by brands, because the bill is account-wide and a per-brand figure cannot tell a real bill driver from noise. `d1_rows_written_account` is the day's D1 rows written summed over every database in the account (no database filter, so Workflow and Durable Object storage and short-lived restore-drill databases count, as they do on the bill); it alerts strictly over 1,500,000 rows a day, under the 50 million a month that Workers Paid includes (about 1.67 million a day). Measured over 14 days the account sits between 7,000 and 45,000 rows a day, with one 227,000 day. `r2_stored_bytes_account` is the day's R2 stored bytes (payload plus metadata, the largest reading per bucket, summed over every bucket in the account); it alerts strictly over 30 GB. That limit is not the 10 GB-month that R2 includes: the account already holds about 22 GB, nearly all of it in two non-0509 backup buckets, so a limit under the allowance would alert every night. It is set above the largest normal day with headroom, so it catches growth rather than the standing overage. The previous per-brand D1 line alerted every night at 57 to 151 rows per brand against a limit of 30, which is a few thousand rows a day, and the guard watched one database and one bucket while the bill covers the account.

For an account-wide breach the `cost_alert` columns `measured_per_brand` and `expected_per_brand` hold the absolute measured figure and the absolute limit, and `on_brands` still records the brand count. The Sentry message names only the day and the lines; the measured figures and limits go in the event's `extra`. The Sentry fingerprint includes the day, so each breaching day opens its own issue instead of joining an earlier one.

The guard evaluates `browser_ms_0509` against the documented 15 browser-seconds per-brand figure (15,000 ms per ON competitor per day, alerting strictly over three times that, like the other lines). The numerator is 0509's own: the Worker adds the `X-Browser-Ms-Used` header of every Browser Rendering response it makes (`browserContent`, `browserScreenshot`, `browserHtmlScreenshot` in `app/lib/site/browser-budget.server.ts`) to a per-UTC-day `BrowserBudget` Durable Object counter named `browser-ms:<day>`, and `runCostGuard` reads that total with `readBrowserMsForDay`. The account-wide dataset (`browserRenderingBrowserTimeUsageAdaptiveGroups`) carries no script dimension, so the guard never reads it: dividing it by 0509's brand count would blame 0509 for other workers' browser time (0509#6086). The counter expires two days after its first write.

The counter sees only what passes through those three functions and only when the response carries the header. No live code opens a Browser Session, so nothing bypasses the counter today: the selector-wait ads transport that did was deleted (0509#7030). A future session leg must report into the same counter, or the line under-reports. The Cloudflare budget alerts at $10 and $20 of total monthly spend (an account setting outside this repo, asserted by no test here) remains the backstop for spend the counter does not see.

The guard runs nightly from the Worker-level `0 3 * * *` cron (`NIGHTLY_CRON`), in the same `Promise.allSettled` as the other nightly work in the `scheduled` handler in `workers/app.ts`, the way #6328 starts the daily Workflows: no new schedule and no new trigger in `wrangler.jsonc`. `runNightlyCostGuard` evaluates the previous UTC day, so the counter is complete. A rejection goes to Sentry through the handler's `captureException`; a breach is one `cost_alert` row (UNIQUE `(day, line)`, so a double fire writes nothing new).

`CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` is optional for the guard. The D1 and R2 lines need the GraphQL analytics read, which needs that token on the Worker. Without it the guard evaluates `browser_ms_0509` and `ai_calls_0509` only, from the `BrowserBudget` counter and from `jev_verdict` rows for that UTC day, which need no token, and the D1 and R2 lines are not evaluated rather than read as zero. Missing the token also sends one Sentry error (`cost-guard-token-missing`) so a D1 overrun cannot stay silent. With the token, all five lines run. The token is a Worker secret, not declared in `wrangler.jsonc`: it is an account-scoped, analytics-read-only token and cannot change or read anything else in the account. The account-wide D1 and R2 storage reads and the snapshot bucket's Class A operations count are one GraphQL request per night.

Each Workers AI line also has a hard daily cap in code, `app/lib/ai/daily-cap.server.ts`: Jev (`JEV_CALLS_PER_DAY`, 20,000 calls, about $3.60 at 16 neurons a call) and the discovery rival proposer (`PROPOSER_CALLS_PER_DAY`, 3,000 calls, about $2.10; each generation takes two calls, one per model, and card-view warming shares the line with the nightly discovery run). A per-UTC-day counter in the `BrowserBudget` Durable Object, the same one the mention allowance uses, is taken before every call; past the cap the call is refused before it reaches Workers AI, Jev reads as unavailable like any other failure, and the first refusal of the day on each line sends one error-level Sentry event (`ai-daily-cap`). A refused call is never retried: discovery counts it as a spend refusal and stops for the day when no other generator returned candidates. One workspace can use at most `JEV_CALLS_PER_WORKSPACE_PER_DAY` (4,000, a fifth of the Jev cap and twice what the largest plan expects: 50 competitors at 40 calls each is 2,000) of those Jev calls a day, so a single heavy workspace cannot leave the others without Jev until 00:00Z; its first refusal of the day sends the same Sentry event with scope `workspace`. The proposer line has no per-workspace share. If the Durable Object counter cannot be reached the call is refused, not allowed (a cap that opens when its counter fails is no cap), and Sentry gets an error-level `ai-daily-cap-counter-down` event, at most once a minute per isolate, line and scope. The workspace share counts one per model request, so a batched request of several questions costs one. Raising a cap is a one-line change that needs Nish's yes with the cost in the PR.

`ai_calls_0509` is 0509's own count of `jev_verdict` rows whose `decided_at` falls on that UTC day. The expected figure is 40 calls per ON competitor per day (the page-role fan-out named in 0509#7066), alerting strictly over three times that, like the other lines. Cached Jev reads do not insert a row, so the line cannot over-report; failed Workers AI calls that never wrote a verdict are not counted.
